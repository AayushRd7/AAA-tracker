Cost, revenue and profit

A tracker holds two sides of a ledger. Revenue is what a conversion is worth — it arrives on an inbound postback and accumulates over the life of a click. Cost is what you paid to get that visitor — it is recorded once, at click time, from the traffic source or from an ad platform. Everything else in reporting is derived from those two numbers:

profit = revenue − cost
ROI %  = (revenue − cost) / cost × 100
EPC    = revenue / clicks

Get cost wrong and every ROI figure is wrong with it, which is why the cost model and the cost parameter matter as much as the offer payout does.

The cost models

A campaign carries a Cost model — the billing basis it is bought on. Choose it under Manage → Campaigns → Add/Edit campaign, in the Costs section.

ModelThe campaign is billed per
CPCClick (cost per click).
CPUCUnique click (cost per unique click).
CPMThousand impressions (cost per mille).
CPAAcquisition — a fixed amount per conversion.
CPSSale — a fixed amount per sale.
RevShareShare of the revenue the traffic produced.
AutoNo fixed basis; let cost arrive from the parameter or the sync.

The model records how the campaign is bought so reports and teammates can read it. Whatever model you pick, the spend that actually lands on a click arrives through one of the three paths below.

Entering cost on a campaign

The Costs section of the campaign editor holds all of the cost settings together:

FieldWhat it does
Cost modelThe billing basis, from the table above.
Traffic loss (%)The expected gap between the clicks the source reports and the visits the tracker records, kept for reconciliation.
CostA campaign-level cost value. Used for the whole period when no per-click cost arrives.
Cost currencyThe currency that Cost value is expressed in.
Cost from cost parameterWhen on, the cost value passed on each click is used instead of the flat campaign Cost.

The cost query parameter

Most traffic sources can pass the price of a click straight through. The campaign link accepts a cost parameter alongside the usual identifiers:

https://<your-domain>/<alias>?cost=0.45&sub_id_1=adset-42

The value is stored on the click itself, so per-click cost can vary by placement, keyword or time of day. For the tracker to read it, map the source's own token to the cost parameter in the source's Settings mapping — for example a token of {cost} on the row whose Parameter is cost. If nothing maps, the parameter is ignored.

Traffic loss

Traffic loss is a percentage, not a deduction. It records the expected difference between the clicks a source bills you for and the visits that reach the tracker — redirects that fail, ad blockers, dropped requests. It is stored per campaign and per source so a reconciliation discrepancy can be explained rather than merely seen. It does not change the recorded cost; it is context for reading it.

Ad-platform cost sync

If you advertise on an ad platform, you can let the platform's reported spend become your cost automatically. The campaign's Ad-platform campaign ID field (in the campaign editor) is what ties the two records together.

  1. Connect the platform under Manage → Integrations and pick the ad accounts to read.
  2. On each campaign, set Ad-platform campaign ID so spend can be matched to it.
  3. The sync pulls each day's spend and impressions, then writes cost onto that day's clicks — the per-click cost is the day's spend divided by that day's clicks.

It is idempotent: re-running a sync replaces the day's allocation rather than adding to it, so running it twice never doubles your spend. Spend for a campaign with no clicks that day is stored but allocates nothing. Use Test mode (dry-run) to compute and log the numbers without changing any costs. The full walkthrough is in Syncing Meta ad spend.

Retroactive cost update

Sometimes the number is simply wrong and you want to overwrite it. In Reports, the Cost update action opens a Retroactive cost update dialog:

FieldMeaning
CampaignOptional. Leave empty to apply to all campaigns.
From / ToThe date range to overwrite.
Cost per clickThe value written onto every matching row. Total spend becomes cost × matched rows.

Every applied change is recorded in Logs → Cost updates, so you can see what was overwritten and when.

The usual mistake

Cost defaults to 0 until one of the three paths supplies it. A campaign with a cost model but no per-click cost, no campaign Cost value and no sync reports 100% ROI on every conversion. If profit looks impossibly good, check cost first — see Cost is missing or wrong.

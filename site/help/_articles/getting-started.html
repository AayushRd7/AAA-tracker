What you are building

A tracker records one row per click and joins the conversions that come back to it later. To get that first row you need three things: a traffic source to label where the click came from, an offer to send the visitor to, and a campaign that ties the two together and hands you a link. Once that link is live, a click becomes a click, a postback becomes a conversion, and the ledger starts to reconcile.

This walkthrough follows the shortest path to a working conversion. Everything else in the tracker — flows, landers, caps, splits — layers on top of these same three objects.

Step 1 — Add the traffic source

Open Manage → Traffic sources and pick your source from the presets, or add it from scratch. A traffic source stores the source's own click-id convention and the macros it understands, so the tracking link the tracker generates already carries the right placeholders.

If your source is not in the list, add it and record how it passes a click id (for example a parameter such as click_id or a source-specific one). Get that wrong and the click still records — it just records without the identifier your source will quote back to you.

Step 2 — Add the offer

Open Manage → Offers and add the destination you are sending traffic to. Set the offer URL — the page the visitor lands on — and its payout. The offer URL accepts macros, and the one you will almost always use is {click_id}, which the tracker replaces with the click id it generated.

Most networks need your click id in a parameter of their choosing. The tracker already appends click_id=… to the offer URL automatically, but the network usually reads a different name, so you add that name yourself in the offer's Target URL — for example &sub1={click_id}. Whatever the network calls it, that is the value it must echo back on the postback.

Step 3 — Build the campaign

Open Manage → Campaigns, create a campaign, and pick the traffic source and the offer you just made. Save it, then copy the campaign link. It takes the form:

https://<your-domain>/<alias>

The campaign link accepts a set of parameters when your source can pass them: click_id, cost, sub_id_1 through sub_id_10, utm_*, external_id, and extras such as country or gender. Anything your source does not send is simply left empty. The alias is the short path you chose; it is what identifies the campaign when the link is clicked.

Step 4 — Fire a test conversion

There is no dedicated test-click or test-postback button. The honest test is the real path: fire a click on the campaign link, then send a postback for it by hand.

Read the click id

Open the campaign link in a browser. Then open Logs → Clicks and find the newest row. Copy its click id — that is the value the network would return to you.

Send the postback

On your own machine, replace the click id and hit the inbound postback endpoint. This is exactly what an affiliate network does when it reports a sale:

curl "https://YOUR-TRACKER-DOMAIN/pb?clickid=YOUR-CLICK-ID&payout=25&status=sale"

A good response is HTTP 200 with a body like {"status":"ok",…}. A 400 means the status or payout was rejected; a 403 means postback security blocked the call. The postback reference lists every accepted parameter and alias.

Where to confirm everything

  • Logs → Clicks — the click you fired, with its click id, sub-ids and UTM values.
  • Logs → S2S postbacks — the inbound call you sent, including any rejection and its reason.
  • Reports → Conversions — the conversion now attached to the click, where you can edit or delete it.

When those three line up, the plumbing is finished. The next useful pages are Offers and offer URLs and Campaigns and flows, which is where routing and multiple offers come in.

In-memory rehearsal

A campaign row also has a Simulate action. It runs the flow, filter and cap logic in memory — side-effect free — without real traffic. Use it to sanity-check routing before you spend.

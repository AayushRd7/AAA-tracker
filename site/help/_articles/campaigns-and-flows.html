What a campaign is

A campaign is your entry point for traffic. It ties a traffic source to the offers and landers you want to serve, and it hands you a public URL:

https://<your-domain>/<alias>

Open Manage → Campaigns → Create campaign and the editor runs as a three-step wizard: Settings · Targeting & rules · Postbacks & review. Only Name and Alias are required — every other field has a sensible default.

Step 1 — Settings

This step holds the campaign’s identity and its cost configuration.

FieldWhat it doesRequired

AliasThe short path that becomes the tracking link — /<alias>. Must be unique.Yes
TypeCampaign (runs flows) or Tracking only (records hits, no routing).No
StatusActive / paused switch. Paused campaigns stop serving.No
Traffic sourceThe source whose parameters and macro convention the link expects.No
DomainWhich of your tracking domains serves the link.No
Flow selectionPosition (first matching flow wins) or Weight % (random split by weight, e.g. 70/30). Also shown as Distribution on step 2.No
TagsFree-form labels for filtering the list.No
Ad-platform campaign IDAd-platform campaign id used by cost sync to match spend to this campaign.No
Cost modelCPC, CPUC, CPM, CPA, CPS, RevShare or Auto.No

There is also a Cost from cost parameter switch: when on, the per-click cost arriving on the campaign link wins over the fixed cost above. The alias is the only part of this step that changes the shape of your link, so pick it first.

Step 2 — Targeting & rules

This step defines the campaign’s flows. Each flow is a routing rule with its own target, filters, schedule, caps and weight:

  • Flow type — forced flows are evaluated first, regular flows follow by position, and a default flow catches everything no other flow matched.
  • Position / weight — used depending on whether Flow selection is Position or Weight %.
  • Target — the offer, lander and/or campaign the flow serves.
  • Filters — geo, device, traffic, sub-id/UTM and time conditions, combined with AND/OR and invertible with “IS NOT”.
  • Schedule and caps — dayparting and per-hour / per-day / total click caps per flow.

Direct vs multi flows and redirect modes

A flow’s schema decides what the visitor gets. The ones you will use most:

SchemaWhat happens
directRedirects straight to the offer URL. Fastest path — no landing.
landing_offerServes a landing page; its click-out link then redirects to the offer.
landing_onlyServes a landing page with no offer click-out.
multiPicks one landing and one offer at random from the configured pools (A/B rotation).
redirect302-redirects to a fixed URL.
redirect_campaignRedirects the visitor into another campaign.
return_404Answers 404 — use as a “drop” flow for unwanted traffic.

A complementary setting, Delivery action, controls how the destination is reached — 302 redirect (the default), iFrame, Form POST, Serve content or Do nothing. The Distribution control at the top of the step is the same choice as Flow selection in Settings.

Step 3 — Postbacks & review

The final step is where you tell the tracker what to send outbound when a conversion lands. Add a Postback URL, choose GET or POST, and tick which statuses should fire it. A traffic source also carries its own S2S postback URL with per-status toggles, and this campaign step lets you add campaign-specific ones. The review pane below summarizes the name, alias, cost model and postback count before you Save.

Note the direction: inbound postbacks (the network reporting a sale to you) go to /pb and are not configured here — see the postback reference. This step is the tracker reporting onward to your source.

Simulate before you spend

A campaign row has a Simulate action. It runs the flow, filter, schedule and cap logic in memory — side-effect free, no redirects and no postbacks — and returns how the traffic would be distributed. Use it to confirm the routing before real visitors arrive. You can pin a country, device, OS or IP prefix for the run.

Two different “modes” people mix up

Flow selection / Distribution decides which flow is chosen (first match vs weighted split). A flow’s schema decides what that flow does (direct to offer, through a lander, multi rotation). One is the selector; the other is the destination. Setting one does not change the other.
